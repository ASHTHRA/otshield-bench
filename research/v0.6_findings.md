# OTShield Bench v0.6 Findings

These findings summarize the completed isolated OpenPLC laboratory study in
[evidence/v06/20260922T021855Z](../evidence/v06/20260922T021855Z/).
[aggregate.json](../evidence/v06/20260922T021855Z/aggregate.json) and
[study.json](../evidence/v06/20260922T021855Z/study.json) are the source of truth
for the results and completion counts. The milestone commit is `17d53ff`;
the execution commit recorded in `study.json` is
`b931b6a850a9a3319327043fe834402a0267187d`.

The study covers Modbus/TCP FC3 traffic under five laboratory connectivity
conditions, comparing baseline `polling-burst-v1` with
`robust-polling-burst-v1`. These are measurements from a controlled OpenPLC lab
with imposed impairments, not observations from production OT systems.
There were **25 trials x 5 conditions = 125 completed condition runs** and
250 completed detector evaluations. Each condition run planned 60 transactions,
for 7,500 planned transactions overall. The study status is `completed`, with
no recorded failed attempts, unattempted runs, or infrastructure failure.

The table reports the mean of each run-level metric across 25 trials per
condition. F1 values are rounded to six decimal places; mean F1 is taken directly
from the aggregate, rather than recomputed from mean recall.

| Condition | Baseline mean recall | Baseline mean F1 | Robust mean recall | Robust mean F1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| clean | 1.0 | 1.000000 | 1.0 | 1.000000 |
| delay20 | 1.0 | 1.000000 | 1.0 | 1.000000 |
| delay20_jitter5_loss5 | 0.95 | 0.973756 | 0.95 | 0.973756 |
| delay40_jitter10 | 0.46 | 0.623010 | 1.0 | 1.000000 |
| delay60_jitter10 | 0.0 | 0.000000 | 1.0 | 1.000000 |

Both detectors achieved recall and F1 of 1.0 in `clean` and `delay20`.
Both had mean recall 0.95 and mean F1 0.973756 in
`delay20_jitter5_loss5`, with no paired improvement for the robust detector.

For `delay40_jitter10`, the paired robust-minus-baseline mean recall improvement
was **+0.54**, with a 95% CI of **0.496218 to 0.583782**. The paired mean F1
improvement was **+0.376990**, with a 95% CI of **0.334642 to 0.419337**.
For `delay60_jitter10`, paired mean recall and F1 improvements were both
**+1.0**; both recorded confidence intervals were 1.0 to 1.0. These are the
aggregate's two-sided 95% Student-t, unbounded run-level intervals, based on
25 paired differences per condition. Zero observed variation within this study
does not establish certainty outside the tested conditions.

Observation coverage remained **1.0** for both detectors in every condition
run, so the recall differences occurred despite complete observation coverage.
False positives remained **0**, and precision stayed **1.0 where detections
occurred**. In `delay60_jitter10`, the baseline produced no detections and the
aggregate records precision as 0.0; this does not indicate false positives.

The resource instrumentation surrounds detector prediction. Memory is Python
`tracemalloc` peak allocation divided by 1024 squared, not total process or
system memory. These small measurements describe the evaluated workload and
host execution; they do not measure full deployment cost, capture and parsing
overhead, network latency, or production throughput. The robust detector's
lower recorded means do not establish a general resource-efficiency advantage.

The evidence boundary and limitations are:

- Isolated OpenPLC lab only, with Modbus/TCP FC3 only.
- No production OT validation.
- No DNP3 experimental validation.
- No EtherNet/IP experimental validation.
- No external replication.
- No universal superiority claim: the observed robust-detector advantage is
  confined to the tested higher-delay conditions; the other conditions show
  equal recall and F1.

This document summarizes retained evidence only and adds no experimental
evidence or changes to the scientific results.
